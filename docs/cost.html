Budgets and limits

01Cost and budgets

Checking costs more than not checking. Ratchet keeps the bill in your hands.

Proving every step uses more AI time than letting an agent write code unchecked. So Ratchet puts hard limits on how much review can happen, spends it where the risk is, and never spends your money on a pay-per-use tool without your exact OK.

C1

Reviewers match the risk

Low-risk steps get one reviewer, and only serious findings block. Normal steps get two, high-risk steps four. You see each step's risk in the plan before you approve it.

C2

The right model for each job

Planning, coding, fixing and reviewing use a strong model. Lighter jobs like planning tests use a cheaper one, and running tests the cheapest. Ask the agent to list them, or change them in the config.

C3

Subscriptions first

Tools you already pay a flat rate for are tried first. A tool that charges per use isn't sent even a test request until you reply pay per use: <model> for that exact model.

C4

Later rounds cost less

Round 1 asks every reviewer. After that, only reviewers with an open serious finding are asked back. A small fix after approval, 20 changed lines or fewer, gets one reviewer unless the step is high risk.

C5

Nitpicks don't buy rounds

From round 3, minor findings stop blocking. They're saved and listed for you at the end, where you decide which to fix.

C6

No paying twice

Two slots that would reach the same company, model and kind of review run once. A round replaced before anyone reviewed it is free, and one flaky test failure costs no attempt.

C7

No paying for tools that are down

A signed-out tool is skipped for six hours. One that says it's out of credits waits for the reset time it gave. What Ratchet learns is shared by every project on your machine.

C8

Repeat mistakes get cheap

When reviewers flag the same kind of mistake twice, the agent offers an automatic check like a lint rule. From then on it's caught in seconds, before any reviewer is paid to find it.

Ratchet counts rounds and attempts, not dollars. The proof receipt reports time per step, never cost or tokens, so check your providers' dashboards for spend.
